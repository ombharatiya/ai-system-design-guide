# Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation is the process of transferring the intelligence from a large, complex model ("Teacher") to a smaller, more efficient one ("Student"). It is a large part of why today's small open-weight models punch well above their parameter count.

## The Teacher-Student Paradigm

Many small models are not trained on raw web data alone. They are trained on outputs, logits, or reasoning traces from a much larger model. The documented cases are open models:

- **Gemma 2** (2B and 9B) and **all Gemma 3 sizes** were pretrained with distillation from a larger teacher.
- **Llama 3.2 1B/3B** incorporated logits from Llama 3.1 8B and 70B during pretraining; **Llama 4 Maverick** was codistilled from the unreleased Llama 4 Behemoth.
- **DeepSeek-R1's distilled checkpoints** are Qwen and Llama models fine-tuned on about 800K samples generated by R1.
- **NVIDIA's Nemotron-Labs competitive-coding model** (September 2026), which scored above the IOI 2026 gold threshold in an unofficial run, was trained on 477,642 reasoning traces distilled from Z.ai's MIT-licensed GLM-5.2. Teacher lineage now crosses vendors and borders, so record it.

Closed labs do not publish small-model recipes. "Claude Haiku is distilled from Opus" or "Gemini Flash is distilled from Pro" is plausible, not documented.

| Model | Role | Intelligence Source |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| **Teacher** | Large (100B+ params, often MoE) | Frontier-scale pretraining (15T-36T+ disclosed tokens) plus post-training |
| **Student** | Small (1B - 30B params) | Teacher's filtered outputs, logits, or reasoning traces |

## How Distillation Works

### 1. Hard Label Distillation
The student learns from the teacher's final predictions (e.g., the answer to a question).

### 2. Soft Label Distillation (Temperature Scaling)
The student learns from the teacher's **probability distribution** (Logits). This is much richer because it tells the student not just the right answer, but which wrong answers were "almost" right.

```python
# Distillation Loss (KL Divergence):
Loss = KL_Div(Teacher_Logits / T, Student_Logits / T)
```
*Where T is the Temperature (typically 2.0 - 5.0).*

### 3. On-Policy Distillation
The student samples its own completions and the teacher scores every token, combining RL's on-policy relevance with SFT's dense signal. It became a popular cheap path to reasoning models in 2025-2026, but its mechanism is now contested: an August 2026 paper (arXiv:2608.31046, revised in September, single group) reports the teacher signal is noisy and that most of the gain comes from suppressing low-probability tokens, which a teacher-free method matched and beat. Run a teacher-free baseline before paying for a large teacher. Also gate on out-of-distribution tasks: a November 2026 study of on-policy distillation for post-RL consolidation (arXiv:2611.05609, Qwen3 models) found students reached 96 to 103% of teachers' in-distribution scores while OOD scores stayed 2.45 to 16.25% below the base model they started from, and covering both teacher capability and data removed that forgetting in their experiments. Details in [RLVR and Reasoning Models](08-rlvr-and-reasoning-models.md#distillation-the-cheaper-path).

## Feature vs. Output Distillation

### Output Distillation (Standard)
Student matches the teacher's text responses.
- **Pros**: Easy to implement via API.
- **Cons**: Only learns behavioral surface patterns. Closed APIs increasingly return summarized or encrypted reasoning, so raw chain-of-thought is usually not available to distill.
- **Platform risk**: Vendor-hosted distillation pipelines are shrinking. OpenAI's stored-completions-to-fine-tune path depends on two products being wound down: Evals was scheduled to go read-only on October 31, 2026 and shuts down November 30, and self-serve fine-tuning stops accepting new jobs from active customers on January 6, 2027, while GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 fine-tunes stop serving on January 14, 2027 (see [Fine-Tuning Strategies](02-fine-tuning-strategies.md#where-fine-tuning-runs)). Keep the dataset, eval suite, and student training on infrastructure you control.

### Feature/Hidden State Distillation
Student matches the inner **Hidden States** (vector representations) of the teacher.
- **Requirement**: You need access to the teacher's weights (Open Weights).
- **Pro**: The student learns the teacher's "internal conceptual map," leading to much higher reasoning depth.

## Self-Distillation via Rejection Sampling

**A core reasoning-training technique.** The literature calls it rejection-sampling fine-tuning, expert iteration, or STaR. The model improves without new human data:

1. **Generation**: The model generates many candidate solutions to a hard math/code problem.
2. **Verification**: A rule-based system (compiler, test suite, calculator, proof checker) keeps only the correct ones.
3. **Distillation**: The model is fine-tuned on the chains of thought that led to correct solutions.

**Result**: The model "distills itself" by keeping only the high-quality reasoning paths. DeepSeek-R1 documents this step: after its RL stage, it collected about 600K verified reasoning samples by rejection sampling (plus about 200K non-reasoning samples) for the next SFT round. OpenAI and Anthropic have not published their recipes. The RL version of the same idea is covered in [RLVR and Reasoning Models](08-rlvr-and-reasoning-models.md).

## Quantization-Aware Distillation

Standard quantization (e.g., 16-bit to 4-bit) causes a small drop in accuracy.
**The Fix**: Use Knowledge Distillation *during* the quantization process. The 16-bit model acts as the teacher, guiding the 4-bit model to minimize its error. Together with quantization-aware training, this is how vendors ship low-bit builds that stay close to 16-bit quality, such as Google's QAT int4 Gemma builds. See [Quantization Deep Dive](07-quantization-deep-dive.md).

## Distillation as a Threat: Extraction via API

If you serve a model through an API, someone will try to distill it. This is now documented at scale:

- Anthropic's February 2026 disclosure attributed over 16M exchanges, through about 24,000 fraudulent accounts, to distillation campaigns by three Chinese labs. Proxy services ran "hydra cluster" account networks (one managed 20,000+ accounts at once), and prompts asked Claude to write out the reasoning behind a completed answer to harvest chain-of-thought training data.
- Anthropic's September 2026 threat report (covered September 10) described nearly 200M exchanges across five attributed campaigns, the largest over 151M exchanges in three months from more than 3,500 accounts.
- In August, researchers showed that encrypted reasoning blocks from several vendors could be replayed across models because each model family shared one encryption key; the vendors closed the hole.
- Hiding reasoning is leaky in other ways too: a September 2026 paper (arXiv:2609.26637) extracted hidden reasoning from frontier models, including GPT-6 Astra, by registering a simple custom tool through a standard API feature.

**Design implications for API operators:** rate shaping and per-account behavioral fingerprinting (including CoT-elicitation detection), stronger account verification, intelligence sharing, and limiting what reasoning you expose, then red-teaming every path that can surface it (tool calls, structured outputs, replayed blocks), not just the response text. Those countermeasures explain several API changes you will meet as a consumer: summarized thinking, and thinking blocks bound to the model and conversation that produced them (Claude Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5; Sonnet 5.5 blocks are also bound to the producing account).

## Interview Questions

### Q: Why is a distilled 8B model better than an 8B model trained from scratch on the same tokens?

**Strong answer:**
Training from scratch (Pretraining) on raw web data is noisy; the model spends a lot of capacity learning to navigate that noise. A distilled model, however, is trained on a "purified" curriculum. The teacher model acts as a high-quality filter, providing structured logic, clear explanations, and a cleaner distribution of language. Essentially, the teacher provides "hints" through its logit distribution that tell the student exactly which features of the language are most important to learn.

### Q: What are the risks of distilling a closed API model into an open-weight student?

**Strong answer:**
1. **Model Collapse**: If the student only sees the teacher's output, it may lose the "long tail" of creative or diverse knowledge and only learn the teacher's narrow biases.
2. **License Violations**: Most proprietary models (OpenAI, Anthropic) have clauses forbidding the use of their outputs to train "competing" models. This is a major legal risk for enterprises distilling their own models from API outputs.
3. **Detection and enforcement**: Vendors now actively detect distillation traffic. Anthropic's 2026 reports describe fingerprinting CoT-elicitation prompts and banning account networks, so a pipeline built on bulk teacher calls can be cut off mid-project.
4. **Less signal than you expect**: Closed APIs expose summarized or encrypted reasoning, not raw chains of thought or logits, so you are doing output distillation only.
5. **Linguistic Mimicry**: The student might learn to *sound* confident (like the teacher) without actually having the same level of logical depth, leading to confident but incorrect hallucinations.

The safer pattern is an open-weight teacher with a permissive license (MIT or Apache 2.0), with teacher identity and license recorded per training row.
